Finasteride Side Effects: The Real Numbers

6 min read · By The Crown Report Research Desk · we may earn a commission from marked partner links (details)

Quick answer

In the original trials, sexual side effects affected 3.8% of men on finasteride versus 2.1% on placebo, a gap under two percentage points. Most cases resolve, and topical versions exist to cut systemic exposure further.

The short version: in the original finasteride trials, sexual side effects were reported by a small single-digit percentage of men, and most resolved after stopping. A minority report symptoms that persist, which you will see called post-finasteride syndrome; it is rare, contested, and worth knowing about. Topical versions produce much lower blood levels, which is the whole reason they exist.

The numbers, without the spin

Finasteride 1mg is among the most studied drugs in dermatology. Across the registration trials, reduced libido, erectile difficulty, and ejaculation problems each showed up in low single-digit percentages of men, with a notable share of those same complaints appearing in the placebo groups too. Most cases resolved after discontinuation, and some resolved while men stayed on the drug.

Less common effects include breast tenderness or enlargement, and mood changes. Any of these are a call to your prescriber, not something to wait out.

Post-finasteride syndrome, honestly

A subset of men report sexual, cognitive, or mood symptoms that persist long after stopping. It is reported, it is debated, and it has no agreed diagnostic definition or accepted prevalence figure. Sites that tell you it is definitely fake and sites that tell you it is common are both overselling what is known. The honest framing: rare, real to the people describing it, unresolved in the literature, and a legitimate reason some men choose topical routes or skip DHT blockers entirely.

The rules that are not negotiable

Finasteride can harm a developing male fetus. Pregnant women should not handle broken or crushed tablets, and topical versions should not contact a pregnant partner's skin. Finasteride also roughly halves PSA readings, so any doctor ordering a prostate screening needs to know you take it. That one matters and almost nobody mentions it.

Why topical exists

Applying finasteride to the scalp produces far lower blood levels than swallowing it, which is the basis for the argument that it may lower systemic side effect risk. The evidence base is younger because these are compounded, off-label formulations, so nobody can honestly promise you zero risk. Our take

For most men, finasteride is well tolerated and it is the only thing that stops the loss rather than just pushing growth. If the side effect profile is what has kept you from treating at all, the topical route is a reasonable trade and that is exactly the market it serves.

Common questions

Do finasteride side effects go away?

In the registration trials most reported side effects resolved after stopping the drug, and some resolved while men continued taking it. A minority describe symptoms that persist, which is the contested area known as post-finasteride syndrome. If anything develops, that is a conversation with your prescriber rather than something to wait out.

Does finasteride affect a PSA test?

Yes, and it matters. Finasteride roughly halves PSA readings, so any doctor ordering a prostate screening needs to know you take it or the result will be read as lower than it truly is.
