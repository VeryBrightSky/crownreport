Does Creatine Cause Hair Loss?

5 min read · By The Crown Report Research Desk · we may earn a commission from marked partner links (details)

The short version: the creatine and hair loss scare traces back to one small 2009 study of 20 rugby players that measured a rise in DHT, the hormone behind pattern baldness. It never measured hair. No study since has shown creatine causes hair loss, and later reviews found no consistent DHT effect. If you are genetically prone, creatine is not what starts it, though a DHT blocker is still the only thing that stops it.

Where the myth came from

In 2009 researchers gave 20 college rugby players creatine for three weeks and reported a meaningful increase in DHT levels. That single result, in a group of 20 men, is the entire foundation of a decade of gym-floor advice. The study did not count hairs, photograph hairlines, or follow anyone long enough to see a pattern change. It measured a hormone and stopped there.

Later work has not reproduced it cleanly. Reviews looking across the creatine literature have found no consistent effect on DHT and no evidence of hair loss as an outcome. That is not proof creatine is harmless for hair, it is proof that the confident claims in both directions are outrunning the data.

What is actually true about DHT

DHT is the hormone that shrinks genetically sensitive follicles until they stop producing visible hair. That mechanism is real and well established, which is why finasteride and dutasteride exist and why they work: they lower DHT. The question with creatine is only whether it raises DHT enough, for long enough, to matter. Nothing published so far says it does.

Worth knowing: if you are losing hair in your twenties, you would likely be losing it with or without creatine. Pattern loss is genetic sensitivity, not a supplement side effect. Our guide on hair loss at 20 covers what onset actually looks like.

What to do if you lift and you are worried

You do not have to choose. If your hair is thinning, the answer is not dropping creatine, it is treating the actual cause: block DHT with finasteride or a topical version, and stimulate growth with minoxidil. That pair works whether or not you supplement, and the budget guide maps it from ten dollars a month upward.

If you want certainty about your own levels, ask a provider for a DHT panel before and after a few months on creatine. That is one blood draw and it beats any forum argument.

Our take

Creatine is one of the most studied supplements in existence and hair loss is not among its established effects. The men who blame creatine are usually men whose genetics were arriving on schedule anyway. If you are noticing thinning, the shed checker and the prevention guide are far more useful than your supplement shelf.

Keep the creatine. Treat the actual cause.
