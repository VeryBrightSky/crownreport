Does Biotin Actually Work for Hair Loss?

4 min read · By The Crown Report Research Desk · we may earn a commission from marked partner links (details)

The short version: biotin fixes hair loss caused by biotin deficiency, which is rare in people who eat normally. If you are not deficient, supplementing does not regrow hair, and high doses can distort thyroid and cardiac blood tests badly enough to cause a misdiagnosis. It is the most-sold, least-effective product in the aisle.

The one situation where it works

True biotin deficiency causes hair thinning, brittle nails, and skin changes, and correcting it fixes those things. Deficiency is uncommon: it shows up with certain genetic conditions, long-term use of some medications, extended raw egg white consumption, and occasionally in pregnancy. If that is you, a provider confirms it with a blood test and treats it.

If your levels are normal, adding more does nothing for your hairline. Extra biotin does not push follicles into growth or lower DHT. Your body excretes what it cannot use.

The part almost nobody mentions

High-dose biotin interferes with a widely used class of laboratory immunoassays. It can produce falsely low or falsely high results on thyroid panels and, importantly, on troponin tests, the marker used to diagnose heart attacks. Regulators have issued warnings about this. Practical takeaway: tell any doctor ordering blood work that you take biotin, and stop it several days before testing if they advise it. That is a genuine safety issue attached to a supplement people assume is inert.

Why it sells anyway

Biotin sits in the sweet spot of marketing: it sounds scientific, it is cheap to produce, it is not a drug so it needs no proof, and hair loss makes people willing to try anything. The gummies at the front of the pharmacy are not there because of trial data.

What to take instead

If you suspect a nutritional cause for shedding, ask for iron and ferritin, vitamin D, and thyroid tests. Those deficiencies genuinely do cause hair loss and are worth correcting. If your pattern is genetic, no supplement addresses it and the prevention guide covers what does. If you are already treating and just want to know whether your shedding is normal, run the shed checker.

Skip the gummies. This is what the evidence supports.
