7 Hair Loss Myths That Refuse to Die

6 min read · By The Crown Report Research Desk · we may earn a commission from marked partner links (details)

The short version: hats do not cause baldness. Neither does shampooing often, styling products, hard water, your mother's father specifically, or cutting your hair. Pattern hair loss is genetic sensitivity to DHT. The myths persist because they offer something the truth does not: a cause you could have avoided.

Hats

The theory is that hats suffocate follicles or cut circulation. Follicles are fed from below by blood vessels, not from above by air, and no normal hat applies enough pressure to matter. The only hat-related loss that exists is traction alopecia from something worn punishingly tight for years, which is not what a baseball cap does. Wear the hat.

Washing your hair too often

Shampoo removes oil and loose hairs that had already detached. Wash daily and you see a few hairs each day; wash weekly and you see a week's worth at once and panic. The total is the same. Frequency of washing does not change the rate of loss, though harsh products can irritate a scalp you are treating.

Only your mother's father matters

A convenient half-truth. There is an androgen receptor gene on the X chromosome, which men inherit from their mothers, so the maternal line carries real weight. But pattern baldness is polygenic and paternal genetics contribute too. Look at both sides of your family, then stop using the folklore as a reason to wait.

Cutting or shaving makes it grow back thicker

Cutting affects the shaft, not the follicle. Freshly cut hair feels coarser because you are touching a blunt end instead of a tapered tip. Nothing about a haircut changes what the follicle produces.

Stress caused it

Partly true, and often misapplied. Major stress, illness, or a crash diet can trigger telogen effluvium, a diffuse whole-scalp shed that begins two to three months after the event and usually resolves on its own. That is real. It is different from pattern loss at the temples and crown, which stress did not start and calm will not stop. Our shedding guide separates the two.

Thickening shampoos and biotin gummies

Thickening products coat the shaft to make existing hair look fuller for a day. That is styling, not treatment. Biotin does nothing without a deficiency, which we cover in does biotin work. Neither touches DHT.

What actually causes it, and what to do

Roughly 95% of male hair loss is androgenetic: inherited sensitivity to DHT that miniaturizes follicles over years. The only things that change that trajectory are lowering DHT and stimulating growth, which is the whole content of the prevention guide and priced out at every tier in the budget guide. Everything on this page is a distraction from starting earlier, which is the only variable you still control.
